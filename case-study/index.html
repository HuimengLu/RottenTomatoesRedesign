Rotten Tomatoes Redesign

Rotten Tomatoes has plenty of information about a movie, but finding what actually helps people decide whether to watch it isn't always easy. I wanted to rethink the movie page while keeping its core value and business model intact.

Interactive PrototypeFigma Link

Rotten Tomatoes has everything a moviegoer needs to know about a film. However, finding what actually helps them make a decision can feel surprisingly difficult.

How might we make Rotten Tomatoes better at helping people decide what to watch?

01Research

Is Rtten Tomates actually broken?

I combined heuristic evaluation with Reddit discussions to understand where the existing experience falls short and what users find confusing.

Heuristic evaluation

The heuristic evaluation revealed two major issues: the ratings were easy to miss because of poor information hierarchy, and ads often competed with editorial content.

1

The first screen lacks a clear visual hierarchy. The Tomatometer and Popcornmeter are difficult to find, even though they are essential to helping users decide whether to watch a movie.

2

Ads are mixed in with movie content, making it harder for users to focus on the information they came to see.

Community discussions

Community discussions pointed to confusion around the rating system, particularly the difference between Critics and Audience reviews and what the scores actually mean.

02Limitations

What needs to be preserved?

Support pre-viewing decision

I chose to focus on the main use case, which is the pre-viewing decision: someone has a particular movie in mind and wants to decide whether it's worth watching before finding where to stream or rent it.

  1. IdeaWhat movie?
  2. Evaluate / validateIs it worth my time?
  3. DecideWhere to watch?

Be mindful of the business model

Rotten Tomatoes is also an advertising and distribution business owned by Fandango. Ads and viewing options therefore need to remain part of the experience.

Make sure RT’s core value stands out

Throughout the redesign, I wanted to keep its two signature rating systems at the center.

04Design Decisions

Key Design Decisions

01

Reorganizing the first impression

I wanted users to get a sense of whether a movie is worth watching as soon as they land on the page. In the original layout, secondary videos took up much of the first screen, while the Tomatometer and Popcornmeter were difficult to find.

I moved the ratings, essential movie information, and primary actions higher in the hierarchy, removed secondary videos, and added a quote from a Top Critic to give users an immediate sense of the film's reception.

I also wanted the page to feel more connected to the movie itself. I used a dark background inspired by its poster and made the trailer the visual centerpiece, creating a more immersive introduction to the film.

02

Separating advertising from editorial content

Since advertising is part of Rotten Tomatoes' business model, removing it wasn't a realistic solution. The real issue was how easily ads blended into the editorial experience.

I reserved dedicated space for advertising so it wouldn't overlap with essential movie information. I also used differences in container width, background, corner radius, and spacing to make the distinction clearer while keeping ads within the page.

03

Giving critics and audiences different voices

One of early explorations

I initially explored a side-by-side layout with similar visual treatments for both review types. However, I realized that readers look for different things in each section, so I chose a vertical layout with distinct visual treatments.

For Critics Reviews, the source is an important part of the review's credibility. I drew inspiration from newspaper layouts, giving more prominence to the publication and original review excerpt.

For Audience Reviews, the opinions themselves matter more than who wrote them. I used a familiar discussion-style layout that puts comments first, gives them more room, and makes space for user interaction.

04

Making reviews more transparent

A single Audience Score can hide very different rating patterns. Two movies could both score 82%, even though one receives consistently high ratings while the other has a much wider spread.

I added the average rating and rating distribution to the Audience Reviews panel, allowing users to see the sentiment behind the percentage.

I also added hover explanations to both Critics and Audience ratings. Since users may not know what each score represents, these explanations make the rating systems easier to understand without requiring them to leave the page.

06Reflection

Reflections

This project turned out to be quite different from what I initially expected. During research, I became interested in a trade-off I hadn't anticipated: how can Rotten Tomatoes support its advertising business without compromising the experience people come for?

The first time I visited the site, I was greeted by an ad for a shoe brand, completely unrelated to movies. It made me think about how irrelevant advertising can disrupt the consistency of an experience and, over time, affect how much users trust the platform.

I came to believe that monetized content needs clear boundaries. In my redesign, I explored this through dedicated ad space and subtle visual distinctions, such as background colors and padding. These small changes help separate ads from editorial content without requiring advertising to disappear.

Given more time, I'd like to explore where that boundary should be. For example, how much visual separation is enough? Could ad relevance be considered alongside placement? And how can we evaluate the impact of these choices on both user experience and ad performance?
